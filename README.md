## My Portfolio

My name is Paula Buitrago, and I am currently in my last year at the University of Maryland studying Information Science. My path to UMD was not completely straightforward. I first attended Montgomery College, where I earned my associate’s degree in Business, and later transferred to UMD. After transferring, I decided to change my major to Information Science because I wanted to learn more about the technology side of business and how the two areas can work together. Throughout my time in college, I have learned that I do not always have everything figured out right away, but I have learned to work hard, be adaptable in different environments, and stay organized even when I have a lot going on. 

Work ethic is probably one of the biggest things that has shaped my college experience. When I first started taking Information Science classes, I did not have a programming background. Classes that involved Python, SQL, databases, and other technical topics were very different from what I was used to studying in business. There were definitely times when I felt overwhelmed or questioned if I was even in the right major. Instead of giving up, I had to learn how to spend more time practicing and asking questions when I did not understand something. For example, in my programming and SQL classes, I would have to go back over material outside of class and work through problems multiple times before they started to make sense. This taught me that working hard does not always mean understanding something immediately. Sometimes it means being willing to sit with something that is difficult and keep working at it. I think this is something I will continue to need in a technology career because I will always be learning something new.

Adaptability has been an important part of my life because I have had to adjust to big changes since I was 12 years old, when I moved to the United States. Moving to a new country meant getting used to a different school system, environment, and way of life while also figuring out where I fit in. Since then, I have gone through other major transitions, like graduating high school, starting college at Montgomery College, transferring to UMD, and changing my major from Business to Information Science. Each stage has been different, and I have had to learn how to adjust instead of expecting everything to stay the same. Even now, I am balancing being a student with working two part-time jobs, which is another environment that has required me to be flexible with my time and responsibilities. Looking back, I think these changes have made me more comfortable with not always knowing what comes next. I have learned that I can enter a new situation, figure things out along the way, and still find my place in it.

Organization is another skill that has become especially important as I have gotten further into college. Since this semester I have to juggle two jobs and six classes, trying to balance school and work at the same time has made me realize that I cannot just keep everything in my head and hope I remember what needs to get done. I have to keep track of assignments, work schedules, deadlines, meetings, and other responsibilities so that one part of my life does not start affecting another. There are definitely days when my schedule feels like a lot, but being organized helps me plan ahead and figure out what needs my attention first. I have also learned that being organized does not mean having everything go perfectly. Sometimes I have to change my schedule or adjust my priorities when something unexpected comes up. I am still working on this because there are definitely times when I procrastinate or underestimate how long something will take, but I have gotten better at recognizing what works for me. I think this will also be important in my goal of becoming a tech consultant, where I will need to manage different projects, deadlines, and responsibilities at the same time. 

Looking back at my college experience, I think these three values are connected. My work ethic helps me keep going when something is difficult, my adaptability has helped me become comfortable with change, and my organization helps me actually manage everything that comes with being a student. My experiences at Montgomery College and UMD have also helped me understand that my career does not have to fit into only one area. My background in business and Information Science has made me interested in the connection between people, technology, and organizations. As I move toward a career in tech consulting, I hope to use the skills I have developed in college while continuing to learn and improve. I know I still have a lot to learn, but I think my experiences have prepared me to keep working hard, adapt when I face something new, and remain organized.
